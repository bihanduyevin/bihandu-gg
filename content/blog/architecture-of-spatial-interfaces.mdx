When we move through a city, we rely on paths, landmarks, boundaries, districts, and visual cues.

Good spatial design makes navigation feel natural.

I think interfaces work in a similar way.

## Paths and navigation

In a city, major roads carry the largest amount of movement while smaller streets provide local access.

A digital interface has a similar hierarchy.

Primary navigation should provide the main route through the experience, while secondary controls provide more local access.

## Landmarks in UI

A landmark is something that helps you understand where you are.

In a digital interface, that can be:

- a strong heading
- a distinctive visual
- a primary call to action
- a recognizable navigation pattern

The important part is consistency.

## Designing for orientation

A user should always have a reasonable sense of:

1. where they are
2. where they came from
3. where they can go next

That idea is one of the reasons I enjoy combining spatial thinking with frontend development.

> The best interface is often the one that makes navigation feel obvious.
